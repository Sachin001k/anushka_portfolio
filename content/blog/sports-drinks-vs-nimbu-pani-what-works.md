Step into any fitness centre or sports shop in Mumbai and you will find bottles of neon colour to replenish electrolytes, enhance stamina and help your muscles recover at a pace far quicker than nature. Price of these are between ₹80 to ₹200 per bottle. In this city, every home has lemons, salt and sugar — the ingredients in something athletes all over the subcontinent have depended on for generations. So which one does work? The first thing you need to learn is what exercise is actually taking away. During sweating, water, salt and minerals are lost from the body and this process occurs quickly, as Mumbai is a very humid place. You also use carbs to fuel your body. This should be the minimum of any drink that is consumed during or after exercising. The food you choose to eat, then, should reflect less your gym bag contents and more your body's needs.

Scientifically designed sports drinks can do just that. They are formulated with a specific ratio of sodium, potassium and carbohydrates to ensure rapid absorption. It's that precision that really counts when it comes to competitive football, long-distance running or back-to-back training sessions for sustained high-intensity effort. The sodium makes you thirsty, the carbs provide fuel for the muscles, and it was meant to work fast.

But the twist is, it's not so simple. Not many of us are competitive athletes. However, a student who completes a half-hour of gym class or a person who takes a walk along the seafront is not in the same class as a marathon runner and thus does not require the same recovery formula. However, the marketing of sports drinks doesn't generally make this distinction. The branding is aspirational on purpose- you are supposed to feel like you are training at the level which is suitable for the product. Most people don't realize there is a significant difference in the audience for these drinks and the people that are purchasing them.

Nimbu pani, on the other hand, has never had a marketing budget and doesn't need one. This lemon juice, water, salt and sugar concoction is one that rehydrates the body quickly, replenishes some of the body's losses due to sweating and is easy to drink when it comes to stomach. It has been doing the job reliably, generation after generation, in climates much hotter than anything that a gym can produce. To be honest, even if you are working out mildly in Mumbai, even walking to the auto can cause your body to get depleted, a well-made nimbu pani can take a lot of beating. It's also a lot more affordable.

The absence of any of these ingredients in either is also a story. It's free of artificial colours, no artificial flavouring, no ingredients that require a chemistry degree to say. This simplicity is a plus, not a minus. There's a reason for the fresh lemon's benefits, and the beverage has little impact on digestion even if your tummy isn't on the right side when you're trying to push through in the heat.

![](/assets/images/blog/sports-drinks-vs-nimbu-pani-what-works.webp)

Consistency is the one thing that is limited in the honest nimbu pani. The ratios vary with each preparation, depending upon the lemon, your hands with the salt, and the quantity of sugar you add. For elite or high-competitive athletes, where accuracy is important, that variability is a problem. Commercial sports drinks do the opposite, as they are always the same.

In most cases, however, it's not really a question of which drink is the more advanced science; it's a question of which drink is better. It's a matter of "matching" to what you actually did. A hard two hours in the heat in the afternoon is a different kind of situation than a light workout followed by the shower. The latter could actually use a sports drink. The latter will do the job very well and nimbu pani, if with a little extra salt, will be okay.

The reality is most people who have paid for a sports drink are actually getting a hefty price tag for a product that their body doesn't require at this time. The lemon in your kitchen is doing the same job, mundane, silently and virtually for free for the everyday athlete in this city.
